# Avoid List — products that look good and aren't

These come up constantly in "winning product" lists for gaming. Each one has a
specific failure mode. Ranked by how badly it ends.

## 1. Retro handhelds preloaded with games ("R36S", "SF3500", "20,000 games built in")

**Failure mode: copyright infringement, store termination, potential damages.**

These are all over TikTok and they are the single most dangerous item in the gaming
dropshipping space. The hardware and the emulator software are legal. The thousands
of preloaded ROMs are not — they were copied and distributed without permission from
Nintendo, Sega, Capcom, and others.

Nintendo won a judgment against LoveROMs/LoveRETRO that included $12 million in
damages, and the court treated selling hardware preloaded with unlicensed ROMs as
contributory infringement. "I just dropship it, I didn't load the games" is not a
defense — you're the seller of record.

**Safe version:** sell the same handhelds with **empty storage and no ROMs**, or sell
officially licensed retro hardware (Nintendo Classic Mini line, Evercade / Super
Pocket, Atari and SEGA official reissues) through legitimate distribution. Margin is
lower and it's a much smaller hook, but it doesn't end with a lawsuit.

## 2. Unlicensed anime / game figures from AliExpress, CJ, Temu

**Failure mode: payment processor freeze, funds held, store shutdown.**

Near-universally bootlegs. The damage usually arrives as a Stripe or PayPal account
review that freezes your balance for 90–180 days, which is fatal for a cash-flow
business well before any lawsuit would be. Covered in detail in
`compliance-and-risk.md`. Use Tier D Routes 1–3 in the shortlist instead.

## 3. Anything carrying a console or franchise mark

**Failure mode: trademark takedown, ad account ban.**

No PlayStation / Xbox / Nintendo / Switch logos, no Pokémon, no Zelda, no Marvel, no
team logos on the product, the packaging, the listing images, or the ad creative.
This trips people up on accessories specifically — a controller dock is fine, a
controller dock molded with a console logo is not.

**Safe phrasing:** "compatible with PS5 / Xbox Series X controllers" as plain
descriptive text is nominative fair use and generally defensible. Using the logos, or
implying you're an official partner, is not.

## 4. Gaming chairs and desks as a launch product

**Failure mode: freight cost and returns, not legality.**

The margin looks great on paper. Then a $180 chair arrives with a scuffed armrest,
and return freight costs more than your profit on three units. Assembly complaints
drive reviews down, and damage-in-transit rates on chairs shipped from Asia are high.

**When it's fine:** month 4+, from a domestic warehouse supplier, once you have the
volume to absorb the return rate. Not first.

## 5. Off-brand wireless earbuds styled as a known brand

**Failure mode: counterfeit seizure and trademark claim.**

Design-patent and trade-dress claims, plus customs seizure risk. There's no version
of this that's worth it. Generic gaming earbuds with a genuinely original design are
fine — clones are not.

## 6. Large-capacity power banks and battery-heavy gear (>100Wh)

**Failure mode: shipping refusal and compliance overhead.**

Lithium cells above 100Wh hit air-freight restrictions, need UN38.3 documentation,
and get held or refused. Sub-100Wh items ship fine. Check the watt-hour rating on
anything with a battery before you list it — this includes some VR head straps with
integrated batteries.

## 7. "As Seen On TikTok" gadgets already at saturation

**Failure mode: no margin left.**

If a product has been running on paid social for 6+ months, CPMs are bid up, every
competitor is on the same supplier, and the price has collapsed to near-cost. Check
ad libraries for how long creatives have been running before you commit. The right
time to enter is early-rise, not peak.

## 8. Anything requiring firmware, drivers, or an app to work

**Failure mode: support load and review damage.**

Macro pads, programmable mice, and RGB controllers are all fine *if* the
configuration software is English and doesn't get flagged by Windows Defender. Verify
this on a sample yourself before listing. A product that works but whose software
looks like malware will generate more support tickets than sales.
